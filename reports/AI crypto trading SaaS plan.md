# Build a Verifiable AI Trading SaaS

**Yes, this is doable** as a Next.js, MongoDB, Auth.js/NextAuth, and AI application: it can find crypto trade setups, sell credits for completed analyses, let an eligible user approve an exact one-click order on a connected centralized exchange (CEX), and provide user and staff dashboards. The launch should cover **spot trading on one CEX in a small allowlist of reviewed countries**, then expand. “Only” those four technologies can describe the application code, but the business still needs exchange market-data and trading APIs, an AI inference provider, payment processing, reliable background jobs, hosting, and secure key management. Funds can remain in the user's exchange account, with trading enabled and withdrawal/transfer access disabled on the connection. The unresolved question is whether the signals have value after fees and missed fills; no research here establishes profitable AI performance. Build a replayable signal scorecard and paper pilot before making performance claims, and obtain jurisdiction and processor approval before accepting payment or enabling live orders. ([Next.js backend guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/backend-for-frontend); [Binance Spot API](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/spot/rest-api); [CFTC AI trading bot advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html))

## Competitors validate the workflow, but leave signal quality unproven

The term **AI trading bot describes materially different products**. WunderTrading supplies an execution layer that a customer's AI agent can control; its Signal Bot also executes external signals. Coinrule converts user descriptions into rules that users inspect and deploy. Cryptohopper explicitly calls its “A.I.” *Algorithmic Intelligence*: it backtests and selects among supplied strategies rather than claiming a generative model discovers trades. Bitsgap's AI configures bot portfolios that subsequently follow fixed rules. Altrady's AI can prepare setups and prefills trades for one-tap confirmation. QuantPilot emphasizes research and backtests, but its FAQ currently names Hyperliquid support and planned CEX integration. These are useful precedents for **explain → inspect → approve → reconcile**, not proof that any vendor's AI predicts profitable trades. ([WunderTrading AI execution](https://wundertrading.com/en/ai-trading-bot); [Coinrule Cloud docs](https://cloud.coinrule.com/docs); [Cryptohopper AI explanation](https://support.cryptohopper.com/en/articles/9133522-what-is-algorithm-intelligence-a-i-and-how-does-it-work); [Bitsgap AI guide](https://bitsgap.com/blog/how-to-use-bitsgap-ai-assistant-set-up-your-first-ai-powered-bot-portfolio); [Altrady pricing and features](https://www.altrady.com/pricing); [QuantPilot FAQ](https://quantpilot.com/))

Established products already show paper trading, journals, P&L, benchmarks, bot statistics, and portfolio views. Coinrule documents an audit log and benchmark comparisons; Altrady lists net P&L, win rate, drawdown, and AI journal review. Altrady and Coinrule also demonstrate visible AI or platform credits, though a “credit” buys different actions on each product. The promising wedge is **a trade ticket linked to an immutable AI thesis, the user's approval, exchange-confirmed fills, and an honest outcome for every published signal**, including skipped, expired, and losing signals. Charge for the defined analysis delivered, not for executing its order; show the credit cost before the scan and refund a failed analysis. Competitor audience figures are self-reported and not comparable active-user counts, so no reliable popularity ranking or market-share claim follows from them. ([Coinrule Cloud docs](https://cloud.coinrule.com/docs); [Coinrule Cloud pricing](https://cloud.coinrule.com/pricing); [Altrady pricing](https://www.altrady.com/pricing); [Altrady credit rules](https://help.altrady.com/en/ai-credits/how-do-ai-credits-work); [WunderTrading home](https://wundertrading.com/en))

## One codebase can run the product, with a worker for market and order jobs

Use **Next.js for the customer and admin interfaces plus authenticated HTTP endpoints; Auth.js with its MongoDB adapter for identities; MongoDB for durable product state; and one TypeScript worker from the same repository for scheduled ingestion, AI jobs, signal outcomes, and exchange reconciliation**. Auth.js documents a MongoDB adapter and application-defined roles. Next.js explicitly cautions that its backend-for-frontend features do not replace long-running backend processes: serverless handlers can be terminated and do not share memory. A low-frequency pilot can use bounded scheduled invocations and MongoDB job leases; continuous market streams or larger order volume need a persistent worker. Vercel cron can overlap, duplicate, or fail without retry, so scheduling alone is insufficient. ([Auth.js MongoDB adapter](https://authjs.dev/getting-started/adapters/mongodb); [Auth.js roles guide](https://authjs.dev/guides/role-based-access-control); [Next.js backend guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/backend-for-frontend); [Vercel cron management](https://vercel.com/docs/cron-jobs/manage-cron-jobs))

The discovery pipeline should ingest **closed, timestamped candles** from a bounded spot universe; reject gaps and stale data; apply deterministic liquidity and risk filters; shortlist candidates; ask the AI for a structured thesis and uncertainty; then validate the output against current exchange rules before publishing a signal. The model must not invent prices or bypass risk limits. Save the source candles and cutoff, exchange/symbol/timeframe, entry range, invalidation/stop, targets, expiry, fee/slippage assumptions, strategy and model versions, prompt hash, and report hash. A signal's “confidence” should be labeled an internal score until outcome data calibrates it. Binance's public candle endpoint and signed trade APIs support this split; its rate limits make shared ingestion and caching important. MongoDB's ordinary collection with a unique `(exchange, symbol, interval, openTime)` index is a simple first implementation; time-series storage can be reconsidered when volume warrants it because MongoDB time-series collections do not provide the same unique-index behavior. ([Binance Spot REST specification](https://github.com/binance/binance-spot-api-docs/blob/master/rest-api.md); [Binance Spot API limits](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/spot/rest-api); [MongoDB time-series limitations](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/v8.0/core/timeseries-collections/))

For **one-click execution**, first render a live order preview with pair, side, order type, quantity/notional, quoted price age, balance, estimated fees, and the signal's expiry and exit assumptions. After the user's explicit confirmation, the server rechecks authentication, country eligibility, key permissions, signal age, balance, exchange filters, quote freshness, and size/loss caps. It records an order intent and stable client order ID, submits once, then derives the displayed status from exchange order queries and fills. A timeout is an **unknown status**, not proof the order failed; reconcile before any retry. Store partial fills, fees, rejections, and cancellation events, with a user and global kill switch. Require the minimum CEX scopes needed for order placement and status/account reads; disable withdrawal/transfer rights, encrypt secrets server-side with a managed key, and audit secret access. Binance documents distinct trading and user-data permissions, caller-defined client order IDs, and ambiguous timeout behavior. ([Binance Spot API permissions and timeout guidance](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/spot/rest-api); [Binance client order IDs](https://developers.binance.com/en/docs/products/spot/faqs/spot_glossary))

Core MongoDB records should include `users`, `roles/permissions`, `countryPolicies`, `exchangeConnections`, `candles`, `scanJobs`, `signals` plus versioned amendments, `signalOutcomes`, `orderIntents`, `orders/fills`, `creditLedger`, `paymentEvents`, `auditEvents`, and `modelRuns`. Use unique idempotency keys and atomic balance reservations; use transactions when a credit reservation and related job record must commit together. The credit ledger is append-only: purchase or grant, reserve, capture when a complete result is delivered, release on failure, and refund/chargeback/adjustment entries. Grant purchased credits only from verified payment events, never from a checkout success page. Keep purchased and promotional balances distinct. These are implementation choices supported by MongoDB's atomicity and transaction model and Stripe's documented webhook/idempotency patterns; **payment-provider approval for this crypto trading product is not established**. ([MongoDB atomicity](https://www.mongodb.com/docs/manual/core/write-operations-atomicity/); [Stripe payment fulfillment](https://docs.stripe.com/payments/existing-customers?platform=web&ui=stripe-hosted); [Stripe idempotency](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests); [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses))

## Analytics must separate signals, simulations, actual trades, and accounts

Display **four distinct performance records**: the published signal, its predeclared paper outcome, the user's exchange-verified fills, and the user's whole-account performance. A signal's hypothetical return is not a customer's ROI. For the signal scorecard, define entry trigger, expiry, fee/spread/slippage convention, partial-fill treatment, and what happens when target and stop are touched in the same candle *before* observing results. Count every published signal, including no-entry, stale, and failed cases; show net return, win rate, expectancy, drawdown, time to outcome, benchmark, cohort, and model version. Use chronological out-of-sample tests and forward tracking; label backtests and simulations conspicuously. This methodology is essential because marketing rules for regulated advisers scrutinize selective and hypothetical performance, and the CFTC warns against AI-bot return promises. Applicability of a particular rule to this business requires counsel's assessment. ([SEC marketing compliance FAQ](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/staff-guidance/division-investment-management-frequently-asked-questions/marketing-compliance-frequently-asked-questions); [CFTC AI trading bot advisory](https://www.cftc.gov/LearnAndProtect/AdvisoriesAndArticles/AITradingBots.html))

For users, calculate **realized P&L from matched exchange fills and actual fees**, and unrealized P&L from open lots marked to a timestamped price source; declare a cost-basis method, currency-conversion method, and period. Show ROI only with its denominator and cash-flow treatment. Whole-account time-weighted return requires deposits and withdrawals as well as trade history; until that import is reliable, the MVP should label charts as *app-executed trades* and avoid claiming whole-account ROI. Attribute an app trade through client/exchange order IDs; a user merely viewing a signal does not prove the signal caused another trade. A full dashboard can then show equity and P&L curves, drawdown, monthly returns, exposure, fees, model cohort outcomes, confidence calibration, and matched-period BTC/ETH benchmarks, with missing-data flags and CSV export. Binance's trade history includes price, quantity, and commission; CFA's performance standards explain why external cash flows matter to return calculations. ([Binance trade-history data](https://www.binance.com/en/academy/articles/how-to-get-account-trade-history-via-api); [CFA Institute GIPS overview](https://www.cfainstitute.org/insights/professional-learning/refresher-readings/2026/overview-of-the-global-investment-performance-standards))

The admin dashboard needs more than aggregate sales. Give staff views of **credit liabilities and reconciliation; payment failures and refunds; scan latency, AI spend and errors; candle freshness; signal outcomes by model cohort; exchange connector and order health; user support; jurisdiction eligibility; and security/audit events**. Separate permissions for support, research, operations, finance, compliance, and admin even if a small team combines jobs. Staff should not silently edit published signals, ledger entries, or performance history: append a correction with actor, reason, before/after value, timestamp, and approval. Require second-person approval for material credit adjustments, performance restatements, and changes to live trading limits. Auth.js provides the role persistence mechanism; authorization still must be checked at every protected endpoint. ([Auth.js roles guide](https://authjs.dev/guides/role-based-access-control); [Next.js backend guide](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/backend-for-frontend))

## A country allowlist and measured pilot determine launch readiness

“Global, with country restrictions” should mean **an allowlist of countries individually cleared for the exact product mode**, not worldwide access followed by a few exclusions. Collect residence and billing country, use IP location as a supporting signal, account for exchange availability, and recheck eligibility at signup, purchase, key connection, and each order. Version policy decisions so support and compliance can explain them. The legal question is product-specific: general market research, personalized recommendations, and discretionary trading can be treated differently. EU MiCA defines advice and portfolio management and restricts who may provide regulated crypto-asset services; the UK also regulates crypto promotions to UK consumers even from overseas firms. A user-approved click does not itself resolve the classification. Have counsel review the first launch countries, the company entity, the asset list, marketing language, and the exchange workflow before paid or live launch. Payment processor acceptance, exchange terms, market-data redistribution rights, privacy retention, and independent security testing are separate gates. ([ESMA MiCA definitions](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-3-definitions); [ESMA MiCA authorization](https://www.esma.europa.eu/publications-and-data/interactive-single-rulebook/mica/article-59-authorisation); [FCA crypto promotions guidance](https://www.fca.org.uk/firms/cryptoassets/marketing-uk-consumers); [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses))

Delivery should follow **four gated increments**. First, decide the initial country/countries, one spot CEX, pairs and timeframes, source-data rights, credit unit, accounting method, and outcome rules; prototype the screener and test an internal paper portfolio. Second, release the scoped discovery-and-confirmation MVP: Auth.js, MongoDB ledgers, Next.js customer/admin views, on-demand scans, immutable reports, a basic forward scorecard, verified credit purchases, trade-only exchange connection, exact-order approval, order/fill reconciliation, audit logs, and kill switches. Live use of that MVP is contingent on the country, processor, exchange, and security gates. Third, add alerts, deeper model and benchmark analytics, exports, and full account import with deposit/withdrawal-aware returns. Fourth, consider additional exchanges or autonomous execution only after a separate operating and legal assessment; futures/leverage are a distinct later scope. **No defensible delivery date or price can be fixed yet** because staffing, hosting, exchange, launch countries, inference cost, and payment acceptance are open.

The pilot should record cost and latency per delivered report, failed/refunded jobs, credit-ledger reconciliation, repeat paid use, support and chargeback rates, signal coverage, and forward results after declared fees. Set thresholds before observing the evaluation cohort. The unit economics test is collected revenue allocated to a redeemed report minus payment, market-data, AI, compute, notification, fraud/refund, and support costs; unused credits are a liability, not evidence of healthy margins. The immediate decisions for the product owner are **first eligible countries and legal entity; first CEX and spot markets; one billable credit action; deployment/worker and payment providers; reference currency and P&L method; and the paper-pilot quality gate**. These choices determine the exact system design and whether the paid, live version can launch. ([Stripe credit-burndown model](https://docs.stripe.com/billing/subscriptions/metered-billing/thresholds); [Stripe restricted businesses](https://stripe.com/legal/restricted-businesses))

## Conclusion

The strongest product is an **auditable decision workflow**: a bounded scan produces a complete, explainable setup; the user sees a current, exact order and approves it; the exchange confirms what happened; the signal and customer outcomes remain traceable. That workflow can be built in one Next.js/TypeScript repository with MongoDB and Auth.js, while a worker and external providers supply the continuous and regulated edges of the service.

The go/no-go test is broader than whether an AI API can generate plausible trades. It is whether the product can deliver lawful access in its first countries, reliable order and credit accounting, a positive cost per redeemed analysis, and forward evidence that its signals are useful after costs. Until those results exist, market the product as transparent trade research with user-controlled execution, not proven autonomous alpha.
